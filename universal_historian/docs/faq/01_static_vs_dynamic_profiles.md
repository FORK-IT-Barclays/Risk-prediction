# FAQ 01: Why use a "Static Profile" instead of "Dynamic Scores"?

### ❓ The Question:
If the world is dynamic and credit scores (like FICO) change regularly, why does this model rely on a "Static" financial profile?

### 🔬 The Technical Answer:
The **Financial Historian** assesses **Structural Risk** rather than **Behavioral Result.**

1.  **Indicators vs. Root Causes:** FICO is a "Result." It changes *after* you have already performed a behavior. Our 13 features (like DTI or Revolving Utilization) are the **Root Causes.** By looking at the debt structure directly, we see the instability **before** the score has time to react.
2.  **Macro-Stability:** A person's salary, total debt load, and active account footprint do not change wildly every hour. This "Static" data represents the **foundation** of their financial life. 
3.  **Long-Window Prediction:** Because the foundation is slow to change, a prediction made today on a static profile is often valid for a much longer period (6-12 months), making it perfect for determining the "Safety Ceiling" of a loan.

**Conclusion:** We don't need to know what you bought for lunch today; we need to know if your financial foundation is strong enough to survive the next 3 years of debt.
